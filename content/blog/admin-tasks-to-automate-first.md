Automation sounds like a big project. In practice, the best place to start is small: one repetitive task that happens every day, follows the same rules and annoys the people who do it.

Here are five that pay back quickly for most small businesses, and what you need to get started.

## 1. Following up new leads

**The problem:** an enquiry arrives through your website. It sits in a shared inbox until someone notices, then gets typed into the CRM by hand. Speed matters: the longer a lead waits, the less likely they are to buy.

**The automation:** when a form is submitted, the lead is created in your CRM, assigned to the right person, sent a personalised acknowledgement and flagged in Teams or Slack, instantly.

**Tools:** Make.com, Zapier or Power Automate, plus your CRM (HubSpot, Pipedrive, Salesforce, Zoho).

## 2. Re-typing data between systems

**The problem:** orders copied from the shop into the accounts package, timesheets copied into payroll, new customers set up in three different systems.

**The automation:** one system becomes the source of truth and pushes changes to the others. Fewer typos, no duplicates, hours saved every week.

**Tools:** native integrations where they exist, otherwise Make.com or n8n.

## 3. Creating and chasing invoices

**The problem:** invoices raised manually after each job, with overdue payments chased only when someone remembers.

**The automation:** invoices generated from completed jobs or signed quotes, sent automatically, with polite reminders at agreed intervals until paid.

**Tools:** Xero or QuickBooks rules, connected to your job management or CRM system.

## 4. Building the weekly report

**The problem:** every Monday someone exports figures from four platforms, pastes them into a spreadsheet and emails it round.

**The automation:** a live dashboard (Looker Studio or Power BI) that pulls the data automatically, plus a scheduled summary email.

**Tools:** Looker Studio, Power BI, Google Sheets with connectors.

## 5. Onboarding new customers or staff

**The problem:** the same welcome emails, document requests, account setups and calendar invites, done from memory each time, so steps get missed.

**The automation:** a checklist-driven workflow that sends the right emails, creates accounts, requests documents and reminds people of what's outstanding.

**Tools:** Power Automate (great for Microsoft 365 businesses), Make.com, or your HR/CRM platform's own workflows.

## How to choose your first automation

Score each candidate task on three things:

1. **How often** it happens (daily beats monthly).
2. **How much time** it takes each time.
3. **How painful** a mistake is.

Start with the task that scores highest but has the simplest rules. Get it working, document it and make sure someone owns it. Then move on to the next.

## Where AI fits in

Large language models (the technology behind ChatGPT and Claude) are now good at the messier steps: reading an email and extracting the details, categorising enquiries, drafting replies for approval, or answering common customer questions on your website. Combined with the simple automations above, they can handle tasks that used to need a person at every step. The key is keeping a human in the loop wherever accuracy really matters.
