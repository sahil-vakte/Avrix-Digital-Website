Cyber Essentials is the UK government-backed scheme that shows you have basic security controls in place. It's run by the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) with IASME as the delivery partner, and certificates are issued by IASME-licensed certification bodies.

If you bid for public sector contracts, supply larger companies or want to reassure customers and insurers, it's one of the most useful certifications you can hold.

## Who needs Cyber Essentials?

Central government contracts that involve handling personal information or providing certain ICT services generally require it. Many local authorities, NHS bodies, universities and large private companies ask their suppliers for it too. Some cyber insurers take it into account.

Even if nobody is asking, it's a sensible baseline. The controls stop many of the everyday, automated attacks that hit small businesses.

## The five technical controls

1. **Firewalls.** Every device that connects to the internet sits behind a properly configured firewall.
2. **Secure configuration.** Default passwords are changed, unnecessary software is removed and devices lock automatically.
3. **User access control.** People only have the access they need, admin accounts are used only for admin tasks, and multi-factor authentication protects cloud services.
4. **Malware protection.** Devices are protected by anti-malware or by allowing only approved applications.
5. **Security update management.** Supported software only, with high and critical updates applied within 14 days of release.

## Cyber Essentials or Cyber Essentials Plus?

**Cyber Essentials** is a verified self-assessment: you answer a question set, a director signs it off and an assessor reviews your answers.

**Cyber Essentials Plus** covers the same controls but adds a hands-on technical audit, including testing a sample of your devices. It gives customers more confidence and is increasingly requested for higher-value contracts.

Most businesses start with Cyber Essentials, then move to Plus when a contract or customer asks for it.

## Common reasons businesses fail

- Laptops or phones running unsupported operating systems.
- Updates not applied within 14 days.
- Cloud services without multi-factor authentication for every user.
- Staff using admin accounts for everyday work.
- Personal devices accessing work email without any controls.

## How to prepare

Start with an honest review of every device and cloud service your business uses, including staff-owned phones that access work data. Fix the gaps before you submit, not after. If you use Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, many of the controls can be applied centrally.
