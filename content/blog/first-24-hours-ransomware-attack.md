A ransomware attack is one of the most stressful things a business can face. Files won't open, systems go down and there may be a ransom note on screen. The decisions you make in the first 24 hours have a big effect on how quickly and fully you recover.

## In the first hour

- **Disconnect, don't switch off.** Unplug affected devices from the network or disable Wi-Fi, but leave them powered on. Memory can hold evidence that's lost when a device shuts down.
- **Don't wipe or restore anything yet.** Restoring from a backup before you know how the attacker got in can reinfect your systems.
- **Don't contact the attackers or pay.** Payment doesn't guarantee recovery and may breach sanctions rules.
- **Call for help.** Contact your incident response provider and, if you have one, your cyber insurer. Many policies require early notification.

## In the first few hours

- **Protect your backups.** Disconnect backup systems and check they haven't been encrypted or deleted.
- **Secure key accounts.** Reset passwords for administrator, email and cloud accounts from a device you know is clean, and check multi-factor authentication is in place.
- **Start a log.** Record what happened, when, and every action taken. It'll help investigators, insurers and regulators.
- **Set up a response team.** Decide who makes decisions, who talks to staff and who handles customers.

## Within 24 hours

- **Understand the scope.** Work with your responders to identify affected systems and whether data was taken.
- **Assess reporting duties.** If personal data is affected and people are likely to be at risk, UK GDPR and EU GDPR usually require you to notify the ICO or relevant EU authority within 72 hours of becoming aware. Organisations covered by NIS2 have an early-warning deadline of 24 hours, and DORA sets its own deadlines for financial entities.
- **Report the crime.** In the UK, report to Action Fraud. Elsewhere in Europe, contact your national police or CSIRT.
- **Plan recovery.** Restore the most important systems first, from clean backups, once the attacker's access has been removed.

## After the incident

Find out exactly how the attacker got in and close that route for good. Then update your incident response plan and run an exercise, so your team is ready next time.
