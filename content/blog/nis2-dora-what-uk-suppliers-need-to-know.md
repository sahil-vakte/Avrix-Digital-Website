Two pieces of EU legislation have changed cyber security expectations across Europe: the NIS2 Directive and the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA). Neither is UK law, but many UK businesses are feeling their effects.

## What is NIS2?

NIS2 replaced the original EU Network and Information Security Directive. It covers a much wider range of sectors, including energy, transport, health, digital infrastructure, manufacturing, waste and public administration, and divides organisations into "essential" and "important" entities.

In-scope organisations must manage cyber risk, secure their supply chains, report significant incidents to tight deadlines and make management bodies accountable, including undertaking cyber security training.

## What is DORA?

DORA applies to EU financial entities such as banks, insurers, investment firms and payment institutions. It sets requirements for ICT risk management, incident reporting, resilience testing and, importantly, managing ICT third-party providers. Critical ICT providers can be brought under direct EU oversight.

## How do they affect UK businesses?

- **EU operations.** If you provide in-scope services in EU member states, NIS2 may apply to you directly.
- **EU subsidiaries.** EU entities within UK groups may be covered by NIS2 or DORA.
- **Customer contracts.** EU customers covered by NIS2 or DORA must manage their supply chain risk, so they're writing security requirements, audit rights and incident notification duties into supplier contracts.

## What about UK rules?

The UK has its own NIS Regulations 2018, and the government's Cyber Security and Resilience Bill is set to expand them, including to more digital and managed service providers. UK financial firms are also subject to FCA and PRA operational resilience rules.

## How to prepare

1. **Check your exposure.** Map where you operate and which customers are covered.
2. **Build on what you have.** ISO 27001 and Cyber Essentials give you a strong foundation.
3. **Get incident reporting ready.** Know who decides, who reports and to whom, within hours, not days.
4. **Review supplier contracts.** Expect new security clauses and prepare evidence to answer them.
